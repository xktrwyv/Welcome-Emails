For decades, Congress has tried—and failed—to pass comprehensive immigration reform.

At the same time, public opinion tells a more complicated story.

See the video

Recent polling shows that:

  • Nearly half of Americans say legal immigration levels should either stay the same or increase, rather than decrease. (Pew Research)
  • Most also say border security should be a top priority. (Pew Research)
  • And more than 7 in 10 Americans say immigration is a good thing for the country. (Gallup)

In other words, many Americans support safe, orderly, and legal pathways to immigration.

So why hasn’t that consensus turned into law?

Beyond the headlines, one question matters: If Americans broadly agree, why hasn’t the U.S. fixed its immigration system?

To understand what’s really behind the gridlock, we sat down with Welcome.US co-founder Cecilia Muñoz, former Director of the White House Domestic Policy Council and one of the country’s leading voices on immigration policy.

Watch now

In this short video, she explains:

  • Why comprehensive immigration reform has been elusive in the U.S.
  • Why past bipartisan efforts have fallen short
  • Why the politics of immigration don’t always match public opinion

The result of this stalemate is more than political—it affects millions of families, workers, and communities navigating a system that hasn’t kept up with reality.

And while the debate can feel deeply divided, the data points to something else: Americans may have more common ground on immigration than it seems.
